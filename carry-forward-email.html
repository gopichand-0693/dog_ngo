Hi [Name],

I’d like to put a small idea in front of you. It needs about fifteen minutes of your time and no budget.

Today, when a Thoughtworker exits, their unused earned leave is encashed and paid out. That money is already provisioned as a liability and is already leaving the company. The proposal is to add one opt-in step to offboarding: the option to convert the value of one day of that balance into a donation to a verified local organisation — a dog shelter or a child nutrition centre — in the city they worked out of.

Working name is Carry Forward, borrowing the leave-policy term on purpose: instead of carrying unused leave forward to next year, you carry it forward to the city you worked in.

  We are not asking anyone for new money. We are adding a checkbox to money that is already moving.

Three reasons I think it’s worth a look

• It costs Thoughtworks nothing. The payout is already provisioned, so there is no new spend to approve and no budget line to defend.
• Exit is our least designed moment. People want to leave something behind. Right now the last thing we send them is a settlement statement.
• Every rupee stays in the city that earned it. Someone who worked out of Pune funds a Pune organisation, not a central fund.

One thing that makes the pilot cheaper than it sounds. The hard part of this is not payroll — it is keeping a trustworthy, current, city-wise list of small organisations. I have a working prototype for exactly that: a searchable directory of 19 dog shelters across Hyderabad and Bengaluru, filterable by locality, by service such as rescue, sterilisation, veterinary care or fostering, and by whether they take emergency callouts. Each organisation has its own page with contact details and hours, there is mobile-number sign-in, and the donation form already captures the earmarked purpose and PAN for 80G. It is a side project rather than a product — payments are not connected and the listings still need formal 12A/80G verification — but it means wave one starts from a real list with real contacts instead of a blank page, in exactly the two cities I am proposing.

What I am not asking for: budget, an HR system change, or a policy amendment.

What I am asking for

1
 A sponsor. One named person from People and one from the Social Change committee, a few hours a week for a quarter.
2
 A legal and payroll read. Can the value of encashed leave be deducted and remitted with the 80G benefit still landing with the employee? This is the one genuine blocker and I’d rather find out early.
3
 Permission to pilot. A manual 90-day run in Hyderabad and Bengaluru with four verified partners — a form and a spreadsheet, no engineering.
On scale, to be upfront At a realistic 20% opt-in this is a few lakh a year, not crores. Its value is that it recurs without approval, costs the company nothing, and gives a few hundred people a year a better way to leave. Opening the same mechanism to leave that lapses at year end for everyone, not just exits, is where the number gets interesting.

One more reason I think it’s worth doing properly: leave encashment at exit is near-universal across Indian employers, so nothing here is specific to us except a willingness to go first. If it works, the playbook — the legal template, the partner vetting standard, the consent wording — is something we can publish for other employers to copy. Thoughtworks going first and giving it away is a better story than Thoughtworks owning it.
